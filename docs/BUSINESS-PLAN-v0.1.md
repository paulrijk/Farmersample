
# FarmerSample.com — Business Plan (draft v0.1)
 · 
## Executive summary
FarmerSample.com (working name) is the showroom of Sri Lankan spices: a proud Sri Lankan company that shows farmers' products to international buyers through graded samples, then sells their harvest. Pepper comes first because it ships in container-size bulk, followed by true Ceylon cinnamon. We start this winter with cinnamon samples and Kandy-region pepper samples (January 2027), build a buyer community around samples, add offers and auctions once buyers are active, aim for about EUR 3M in traded value in year 2, and expand to Kerala, India, in year 3. Farmers sell us raw pepper; we dry and machine-process it ourselves, because processing decides the quality. That lets us offer black, white and coloured pepper, plus white-label grinding and packing.
Problem: farmers sell to a few local traders at local prices, and much high-quality Ceylon pepper leaves via Indian traders at a discount. International buyers have no easy, trusted way to buy direct.
Solution: a hybrid live and online auction platform (WeAuction technology), with a processing and grading hub, a lab report per lot, paid sample kits, escrow and export logistics.
Why us: Saj, our Sri Lankan partner, brings sourcing, warehousing, certified plantations and direct contacts at the Export Development Board (EDB). Paul brings the auction technology and European buyers.
Revenue: buyer and seller commission (around 6% combined), sample boxes sold with auction registration, buyer membership, processing and white-label packing, grading, storage and logistics services, and later pre-financing and an own trading book.
Illustrative numbers: EUR 1M traded value in year 1, EUR 3M in year 2, EUR 8M in year 3, with about EUR 57k operating profit in year 2. All figures are assumptions to be checked.
This is draft v0.1, built on assumptions from the first brainstorm. Numbers marked as assumptions need confirming with Saj and with buyers.
## Problem and opportunity
Sri Lanka grows some of the world's best spices, but the trade is fragmented and opaque. The gap between farmgate and export prices goes mostly to intermediaries.
For farmers and small processors
They sell at the village level to whichever trader comes by. There is no central marketplace or public price for spices.
A few large players try to buy up as much as possible during the season, which keeps farmgate prices low.
Many farmers have no access to buyer ins at all. Even simple matchmaking would add value.
Storing stock is profitable but needs capital. Dried pepper bought at about LKR 1,500/kg in season has sold for about LKR 3,200/kg four months later.
For international buyers
It is hard to find reliable sellers and to check quality before buying.
Samples are asked for case by case.
Quality and residue risk sits with the buyer. Pesticide residue limits are a common reason EU spice shipments get rejected.
The opportunity
Ceylon pepper has a quality premium thanks to its high piperine content and high density (above 550 g/L). Yet in early 2026 Sri Lankan pepper traded around USD 7,200/t against about USD 8,650/t for Indian pepper, and India is Sri Lanka's largest pepper buyer.
Sri Lanka supplies most of the world's true (Ceylon) cinnamon, so it has real pricing power there.
Auctions are already familiar in the region. The Colombo Tea Auction sells graded, sampled lots every week, and India's Spices Board runs e-auctions for cardamom. No open, international, sample-first auction exists for pepper, cinnamon and other dry spices.

Pepper processing and product lines. Farmers sell raw pepper to Saj Company; we dry and process it at the hub. Processing decides the quality. From the same raw pepper we can make standard black pepper, white pepper (black husk removed), and red and other coloured pepper, which are now popular. Cinnamon supply and grades. Cinnamon is cut every 3-4 months all year, except in the rainy season, so samples can be sent straight away. The grade (Alba is the thinnest quill, thinner than a pencil, then C5, C4 and lower) depends on the skill of specialist cutters. Price reference points (2026). Selina Wamucii put the Sri Lankan farmgate pepper price at USD 5.90/kg in June 2026. Indian black pepper 500 g/L was offered at around EUR 5.4-7.9/kg FOB across 2026. Saj's season and off-season prices fit these levels.
Target buyers. These are spice importers, grinders and packers, food manufacturers, specialty retailers and private-label brands (for white-label ground and packed pepper) in the EU (Spain, Germany, Netherlands, UK), the US and the Gulf. The Middle East is a very large spice market to approach early, and award-winning lots can reach 5-star hotels and restaurants. Ten committed buyers are enough to start.
Kerala, India (phase 3). Kerala is a much bigger spice region with similar village-level trading. Paul knows a small, well-connected farm in Kerala that could be our first partner there. Like Saj in Sri Lanka, we will need a local person who knows the market and can do the groundwork.

## Solution: how the marketplace works
Every lot passes through a grading hub before it is listed, so buyers bid on something they have tested themselves. The platform runs on WeAuction's white-label hybrid auction technology and can be live within weeks.
Samples go out before the auction, and the farmer is paid from escrow once the goods ship. Buyers can buy through three formats: fixed-price catalogue offers, timed online auctions, and live hybrid auctions streamed from the hub in peak season. For domestic traders, the same platform runs local auctions in LKR near the farms, which builds supply and a public reference price.
## Growth strategy: start with samples
We start with a sample showroom, not auctions: farmer profiles in a web app where international buyers order samples and make offers. Samples are where buyer trust begins, and this step proves demand at low cost before we build escrow, logistics and auctions.
How the sample showroom works
Farm visits. The Export Development Board (EDB) shares its data on plantations, hectares and farmers, and the regional agricultural officers take Saj's team to the farms. The message is simple: "we are a proud Sri Lankan company showing your product to international buyers." The team takes photos and the farm story, records volumes, grades, harvest period and certifications, gives each farm a QR code, and collects 1-2 kg of sample per product.
Process, grade and seal. Farmers deliver raw pepper. At the hub it is dried (6-7 days) and processed into black, white or coloured pepper: by hand for small sample quantities, by machine (destoning, dust removal, size grading) for bulk, then each sample is graded with moisture meters and density checks, and split into numbered, sealed pouches with the farm's QR code. Lab tests follow only when a buyer is serious. From farm to sample in Spain takes about 2 weeks.
Farmer profiles. The web app shows each farmer with products, available volume, harvest period, certifications, photos and an indicative price range. Each sample shows a "valid until" date and the volume behind it.
Order samples. Buyers order single samples or a "Sri Lanka discovery box" with several farmers.
Make offers. Buyers send an offer through the platform (product, volume, price, delivery). FarmerSample and Saj negotiate and close the deal.
Ship the order. Saj, or a licensed exporter, is the exporter of record and ships the goods.
Design choices
A European sample stock. One consolidated air-freight sample shipment a month goes to Europe (Paul in Spain, or a small fulfilment partner). EU buyers then get samples in 1-3 days without customs on every parcel. Import rules for commercial food samples need checking first.
Profiles without contact details. Buyers see the farm, region, story and quality data, but all contact and offers run through FarmerSample. This protects the commission.
Samples are paid, then credited. A discovery box costs about EUR 50-150, credited against the first order. This filters out time-wasters and covers sample costs.
A closed buyers group. We invite 10-20 importers, grinders and specialty retailers. They get a discovery box of new farmers every quarter, and later become the first auction audience.
Bundling small farms. Most smallholders cannot fill an export order alone. FarmerSample combines farms of the same grade into one lot, which is a core part of our value.
Sourcing through the Export Development Board. The EDB has government targets to bring international buyers to Sri Lankan plantations, so our proposal helps them reach those targets. Saj knows several EDB directors through his coffee work. The EDB also runs training for farmers and exporters (GMP, USDA organic), which helps suppliers reach export standard. This means we do not need to find suppliers one by one.
Speed from sample to sale. Farmers harvest in batches over about 2 months (Saj bought 18 t of cacao pods from one farm over 2 months this season). So once a sample reaches buyers, we need an offer within about 1 to 1.5 months, or the crop goes to local traders. Later, for repeat buyers, we buy the crop and store it in Saj's warehouse, and eventually keep ready stock in the EU, so turnaround drops to days.
Quality expertise is part of the price. Buying direct from farms carries real risks, such as adulterated pepper. Our grading, testing and knowledge of the trade's tricks protect buyers, and we handle shipping, logistics and customs so the buyer only pays and receives.
Community first. Like Catawiki, which started as a blog before it became an auction house, we build the community first. Samples bring buyers and farms together. Offers follow, and once enough buyers have tasted the same lots (for example 200 sample orders), we run an auction.

Unit example (assumption). A 20 ft container holds about 12-13 t of bagged pepper (Saj's figure), worth about EUR 75k FOB. At 6% commission that is about EUR 4.5k per container. ## Brand, trust and certifications
The brand promise is proof, not a logo: every lot shows its farm, its lab report and how much of the hammer price went to the farmer. Proof builds trust with buyers and also avoids the legal risk of making our own "fair" claims.
Name. FarmerSample.com is the working name. It says what we do first: show farmers' products and send their samples to buyers. It also avoids the word "fair": "Fairtrade" is a protected certification mark, and EU rules on green and social claims (Directive 2024/825, applying from autumn 2026) restrict self-made sustainability labels. Next: secure the domain and check trademarks in the EU, UK, US, Sri Lanka and India. Once auctions start, the name still fits, because every lot is sold on its sample. Paul and Saj both like it because it says exactly what we do. Saj checks the Sri Lankan trademark; Paul checks the .com and the EU trademark. A second, heritage-style name for auctions or awards, with the authenticity of the Colombo Tea Auction, is still open.
Trust built into each lot
Processing and grading at intake: drying, destoning, dust removal and size grading (by machine for bulk, by hand for samples); then moisture, density (pepper above 550 g/L), cleanliness, size grade, grade (for example cinnamon Alba, C5, C4).
Lab report: piperine or oil content, pesticide residues against EU and US limits, aflatoxin and microbiology.
Sealed, numbered samples taken from the same lot. If the delivery does not match the sample, FarmerSample mediates and the buyer gets a refund.
Escrow: the buyer pays FarmerSample, and the seller is paid when the goods are shipped or accepted.
Farm profile: photos, family story, location, certifications, and the share of the price paid to the farmer. Each farm gets a QR code that links to its profile and can later go on a sign at the field; Sri Lankan farmers already know QR codes from the fuel-quota system.
Existing certifications to build on (Saj's plantations already hold several of these): organic (EU organic "Euro-leaf" logo, USDA Organic), Rainforest Alliance, GMP, the EU geographical indication for Ceylon Cinnamon, and FDA facility registration for the US.
Impact story. Local farmers, international prices, fewer middlemen. Community work such as Saj's school donation campaign for about 350 children near his coffee factory goes on the platform as real stories with photos.
## Go-to-market and buyer acquisition
We need only about 10 active international buyers and 50 reliable suppliers to make the pilot work. We win buyers one visit at a time and grow supply through Saj's existing network.
Buyers (led by Paul)
Visit 10-15 importers and grinders in Spain, the Netherlands and Germany, starting with Dani near Cabrils.
Give pilot buyers free membership and their first sample boxes free, and run the first auctions with a fixed small group of invited buyers. Paid membership starts in year 2.
Attend trade fairs: Biofach (organic, Nuremberg), SIAL or Anuga (food), and the Sri Lanka Export Development Board's spice promotions.
Approach buyers who already import Sri Lankan pepper and cinnamon, found through customs data (Tridge, TradeInt).
Suppliers (led by Saj)
Start with plantations that already hold organic, Rainforest Alliance or GMP certification.
Add smallholder groups through collection points near Saj's warehouses.
Run local hybrid auctions in LKR for domestic traders. This builds steady supply and a public reference price.
Selling format, step by step
Sample showroom: farmer profiles, paid samples and discovery boxes, and offers from buyers, closed as negotiated deals (see Growth strategy).
Once there are 5 or more active buyers: timed online auctions every two weeks.
In peak season: live hybrid auctions streamed from the hub, with local and international bidders.
Flagship competition (from year 2). An annual award (working name "Ceylon Spice Awards") judged by an international jury of chefs, spice buyers and labs. Winning lots are sold in a special auction, which brings press, sets top prices and draws new buyers into the regular auctions. Awards give hard-working farmers recognition and build our reputation, as award-winning coffee lots show, and attract 5-star hotels and specialty buyers.

## Roadmap: Sri Lanka pilot to Kerala
We prepare this winter with plantation visits, a first website and cinnamon samples, open the showroom in January 2027 with Kandy-region pepper, add the main May crop, add escrow, logistics and auctions from late 2027, and enter Kerala in 2029. Each step happens only once the gate before it is passed.
The pepper windows follow the harvests Saj described: Gampola/Kandy from December, Bibile and Middeniya in May, and the hill country in September. Cinnamon can be sampled all year.
